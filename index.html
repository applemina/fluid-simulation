FLUID MECHANICS · GRAVITY FLUSH DEMO

What does it take to get your shit down?

A gravity-flush toilet is a three-stage fluid system: a tank stores hydrostatic energy, a valve converts it into a jet, and a ring of rim holes distributes that jet around the bowl to scour it clean. Tune the tank, the valve, the liquid, and the rim geometry below to see whether the flush actually clears the bowl or just dribbles and fails.

Energy Source — Tank & Flush Valve
Bernoulli's equation between the free surface (v₁) and the valve exit (v₂)

Flow through the valve follows Bernoulli's equation between the free surface and the valve exit — corrected for the surface's own motion, the backpressure waiting downstream, and the fluid's viscosity.

Explanation

1 · Two points, one energy balance

Bernoulli's equation says energy per unit volume is conserved along a streamline between the free surface (1) and the valve exit (2): pressure, kinetic, and potential (height) energy trade off against each other.

2 · The surface isn't standing still

As water leaves through the valve, the surface itself drops — it has some velocity v₁ too. Continuity (mass in = mass out) ties it to the exit velocity through the ratio of the two cross-sectional areas: a wide tank makes v₁ tiny, a narrow one doesn't.

3 · The exit isn't at atmospheric pressure

Downstream of the valve there's usually standing water or resistance from the bowl/trapway. Rather than assuming the exit is open to air (gauge pressure zero), that resistance is modeled as an equivalent backpressure head Hback the jet has to push against.

5 · One more correction: viscosity

v₂ above is still the frictionless ideal. Real fluid drags against the orifice wall, and that drag matters more at low Reynolds number (thick or slow-moving fluid). A discharge coefficient Cd — approaching a fixed maximum at high Re, falling off as Re drops — scales v₂ down to the actual exit speed.

Distribution Network — Rim & Holes
Flow splits across N rim holes; each jet imposes wall shear on the ceramic

Conservation of flow: water enters the rim channel at a single point at the top (start) and discharges through each hole in turn, so the flow — and velocity — remaining in the channel decreases monotonically as it travels around the rim (see the fading ring color and the start/end channel-velocity readouts). Rim cavity sets the channel's own cross-section: a narrower cavity means higher channel velocity for the same Q. Hagen–Poiseuille wall shear: τwall = 4μvhole/rhole at each exit jet. Reynolds number (gauge, right) tells you the flow regime at the rim jets — see the three zones below.
